How to review

These questions come from DrawEduMath: photos and scans of students’ handwritten math work, described by teachers. The questions and answers you’ll see were written by teachers. Most of the AI models we tested answered them wrong. We need to know whether that’s because the question is genuinely hard for AI, or because something is wrong with the question or its answer.

For each one, look at the student’s work (right side of the image; the problem is on the left), read the question and the teacher’s answer, then choose:

Approve

The question can be answered from the student’s work, a teacher would agree on the answer, and the teacher’s answer shown is correct.

Reject

The question isn’t worth keeping, even with edits: it can’t be answered from the image, it’s too subjective for teachers to agree on, or it asks about something trivial. For example, "Unclear" is not a good answer. Write one line saying why you rejected it.

Modify

The question is worth keeping but needs fixing: the wording is ambiguous (for example, “the first line” when there are two), the teacher’s answer is wrong or incomplete but can be fixed, or the idea is promising but the implementation is poor. Rewrite the question, the answer, or both. Your version goes back into the queue for another reviewer. If you're asked to review a question that you re-wrote, skip it.

Skip image

The image goes back to the queue for someone else.

Model answers

Each model’s answer is shown with the rating AI judges gave it against the teacher’s answer: 4 same meaning, 3 correct but partial, 2 wrong, 1 irrelevant or wrong. Use them to spot a teacher answer that might be wrong or a question that reads two ways, not as the right answer.
